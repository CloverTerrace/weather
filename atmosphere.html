What does this mean?

CAPE ("Convective Available Potential Energy") is a measure of how much fuel is available for a rising thunderstorm updraft — think of it as the size of the tank, in Joules per kilogram of air. Higher numbers mean a storm that does form can grow taller and stronger. CIN ("Convective Inhibition") is the opposite: a layer of warm air aloft acting like a lid, holding storms down until something forces air through it. Some CIN is normal and even helpful — it lets energy build up before storms break out.

2500–4000 J/kgStrong

What does this mean?

Shear is how much wind speed and direction change with height — strong shear organizes a storm's updraft and downdraft so they don't choke each other out, which is what lets a storm grow big and long-lived instead of collapsing after 20 minutes. Storm-Relative Helicity (SRH) measures how much that changing wind would make an updraft spin, relative to how a storm itself is expected to move. High SRH in the lowest kilometer or so is the ingredient most closely tied to tornado potential.

What does this mean?

Lapse rate is how fast temperature drops as you go up. A steep mid-level (700–500mb) lapse rate means the air a couple miles up is unusually cold relative to the surface — that temperature difference is what lets warm surface air keep accelerating upward instead of leveling off, which is part of why steep lapse rates favor stronger updrafts and larger hail.
